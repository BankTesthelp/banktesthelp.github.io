Bank Jobs in Pakistan 2026: Banks, Eligibility and How the Tests Work

Banking remains one of the most sought-after career paths for Pakistani graduates: structured grades, clear promotion ladders, and hiring that — at least at entry level — runs largely on open advertisement and written merit. This overview explains the landscape for 2026: which banks hire, the typical eligibility candidates need, the entry routes into the industry, how recruitment tests are organised, and how to apply and prepare. It is a general guide to how bank hiring works — always rely on each bank's official advertisement for the exact criteria of any specific opening.

Which Banks Hire in Pakistan?

The employers fall into a few groups. At the centre sits the State Bank of Pakistan, the regulator, which inducts officers through schemes such as SBOTS. The large public-sector and provincial banks include the National Bank of Pakistan, the Bank of Punjab, Bank of Khyber and Sindh Bank. The big private commercial banks — HBL, MCB, UBL, Meezan Bank, Allied Bank, Askari Bank, Bank Alfalah, Faysal Bank, JS Bank, Soneri Bank and others — run the largest and most frequent hiring programmes. Meezan, as the largest Islamic bank, anchors the Islamic banking sector, where a few other full-fledged Islamic banks and Islamic windows of conventional banks also hire. Between them, these institutions advertise trainee programmes, officer inductions and branch-staff positions throughout the year.

Typical Eligibility — General Guidance

Requirements differ by bank and role, but the broad pattern is consistent. For officer and trainee programmes, banks typically ask for sixteen years of education — a bachelor's or master's degree from an HEC-recognised institution — often with a minimum division or CGPA stated in the advertisement. For branch operations, cashier and services roles, a bachelor's degree (fourteen to sixteen years of education depending on the role) is commonly accepted. Trainee programmes usually carry an upper age limit somewhere in the mid-to-late twenties, while experienced-hire roles set their own ranges. Some roles specify disciplines — business, commerce, economics, mathematics, statistics or computer science appear often — while many trainee programmes accept all disciplines. Treat these as general guidance only; the advertisement for the opening you want is the only version that counts.

Entry Routes into Banking

  • Management Trainee / Trainee Officer programmes: the premium route — a structured programme leading to officer grades, open mainly to fresh graduates.
  • Officer grades (OG-I, OG-II, OG-III): direct officer inductions, including the State Bank's SBOTS (OG-2) and NBP's officer hiring.
  • Branch roles: Personal Banking Officers (Meezan's PBO is the best-known example), Branch Services Officers, cashiers and tellers, customer services officers.
  • Sales roles: retail executives and liability/asset sales officers, usually target-driven.
  • Specialist roles: IT, risk, audit, treasury, trade and compliance positions for candidates with matching qualifications.

How Bank Recruitment Tests Work

Most banks shortlist through a written test, conducted either by the bank itself or through a testing service — organisations such as NTS, PTS, OTS and IBP have all conducted bank tests, and several banks run their own online assessments. The typical paper is MCQ-based and covers English, quantitative ability, IQ and reasoning, general knowledge and current affairs, and banking awareness, with the mix and length varying by bank. Our pattern pages break this down bank by bank: start with the SBOTS pattern, the Meezan PBO pattern, the UBL pattern, the NBP pattern and the HBL pattern. Candidates above the cut-off are called for interviews, and some programmes add group discussions or assessment centres. Final merit combines the stages as the bank's policy states.

What Banks Look For Beyond the Test Score

Clearing the written test gets you into the room; it does not by itself get you the job. In interviews and group exercises, banks consistently look for clear spoken communication in Urdu and English, basic computer confidence — email, spreadsheets, data entry — a presentable, professional manner, and genuine willingness to accept postings and rotations, including outside your home city. Candidates from any academic background succeed when they can explain, simply and honestly, why they want banking and what they understand about the role. None of this requires connections or money; it requires the same preparation habit as the test itself, applied a few weeks earlier than everyone else applies it.

How to Apply — and How to Prepare

Openings are announced on banks' own careers pages, in national newspapers, and on job portals; serious candidates check the careers pages of their target banks weekly and keep scanned documents — CNIC, photographs, degrees and transcripts — ready, because application windows are often short. Preparation, fortunately, does not need to wait for an advertisement: the test pattern barely changes, so the candidates who start early win. Bank hiring in Pakistan rewards exactly one thing at entry level — merit demonstrated in a timed test — and that is a skill anyone can build with steady, structured practice.
