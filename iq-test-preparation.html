IQ and Reasoning Test Preparation with Worked Examples

The IQ and reasoning section frightens beginners more than any other part of a bank test — and it is actually the easiest section to improve. IQ questions are not testing genius; they test whether you recognise a small set of repeating patterns quickly. Once you have seen each question family and understood its logic, the same patterns return in test after test wearing different numbers. This guide explains the main families with original worked examples. Solve each example yourself before reading the solution — that struggle is where the learning happens — then drill with the timed quizzes on our topics page.

Family 1: Number Series

Example 1: 3, 6, 12, 24, ? — Each term doubles the previous one: 3 × 2 = 6, 6 × 2 = 12, 12 × 2 = 24, so the next term is 24 × 2 = 48. When a series grows fast, test multiplication and division first.

Example 2: 5, 9, 17, 33, ? — Look at the differences: 9 − 5 = 4, 17 − 9 = 8, 33 − 17 = 16. The differences themselves double (4, 8, 16), so the next difference is 32 and the next term is 33 + 32 = 65. If a series grows steadily but not by multiplication, write the differences underneath; if those are not constant, check whether the differences form their own pattern. Other common engines are adding consecutive numbers, squares (1, 4, 9, 16…), cubes, and alternating operations such as ×2 − 1.

Family 2: Letter Series

Example 3: B, D, G, K, P, ? — Convert letters to positions: B = 2, D = 4, G = 7, K = 11, P = 16. The jumps are +2, +3, +4, +5, so the next jump is +6, giving 22 = V. Almost every letter series becomes a number series once you write the alphabet positions — memorise the landmarks A=1, E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Z=26 and count from the nearest one.

Family 3: Analogies

Example 4: Book : Pages :: Wall : ? — First state the relationship in the given pair in one sentence: "A book is made of pages." Apply the same sentence to the second pair: "A wall is made of…" — Bricks. Analogy questions fail when candidates match surface similarity instead of the relationship. Always name the relationship first — part–whole, worker–tool, cause–effect, synonym, antonym, degree — and only then choose the option that fits the same relationship.

Family 4: Coding–Decoding

Example 5: If CAT is coded as 24, what is the code for DOG? Test the simplest hypothesis: letter positions added together. C = 3, A = 1, T = 20, and 3 + 1 + 20 = 24. ✔ The rule works, so apply it: D = 4, O = 15, G = 7, and 4 + 15 + 7 = 26. Other common rules are positions written directly, letters shifted forward or backward by a fixed number (A→C is +2), and reversed alphabet positions (A = 26, B = 25…). When one hypothesis fails on the given example, discard it immediately and test the next — never force it.

Family 5: Blood Relations

Example 6: A man says, pointing to a girl: "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." Who is the girl to the man? Unpack from the inside out: "my mother's only son" — the mother has one son, and the speaker is a man, so that son is the speaker himself. The girl is therefore the daughter of the speaker: his daughter. The method for every blood-relation question is the same: replace each phrase with the actual person, step by step, and draw a tiny family sketch if the chain is long. Mark generations vertically and marriages horizontally, and the answer usually becomes obvious.

Family 6: Odd One Out

Example 7: Apple, Mango, Banana, Potato — three are fruits; the potato is a vegetable, so Potato is the odd one out. The hidden category can be anything: fruits versus vegetables, metals versus non-metals, prime versus composite numbers, or words that share a spelling pattern. If your first category makes two items odd, it is the wrong category — look again.

How to Practise IQ Questions Properly

Work in short, timed sets rather than long marathons: fifteen questions, then a full review of every mistake, is worth more than fifty questions glanced at. In the test itself, give a series question at most a minute; if no pattern appears, mark it and move on — easier marks are waiting later in the section. Keep a notebook of every pattern that beat you, organised by family; after a few weeks you will notice your notebook stops growing, which is precisely when this section turns from your weakest into your most reliable source of marks.
