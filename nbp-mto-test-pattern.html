NBP MTO and OG-II Test Pattern and Syllabus

The National Bank of Pakistan is the country's largest public-sector bank, and its Management Trainee Officer (MTO) programme and Officer Grade-II (OG-II) inductions are among the most applied-for openings in Pakistani banking. Thousands of graduates compete for a limited number of seats, and the written test does most of the filtering. This page explains the typical NBP MTO and OG-II test pattern, the syllabus beneath each section, and how to prepare in a structured way. As always, the official advertisement for the specific batch is the final authority — the pattern below reflects the typical structure of these tests in recent years.

The Typical Test Pattern

NBP entry-level tests are generally MCQ-based and timed, assembled from five areas:

  • English Language — comprehension, grammar and vocabulary, usually a substantial block.
  • Quantitative Ability — arithmetic, basic algebra and data handling.
  • IQ and Analytical Reasoning — series, arrangements and logical problems.
  • General Knowledge and Current Affairs — Pakistan studies, world affairs and recent events.
  • Banking, Finance and Pakistan Economy — banking terms, the role of the State Bank, and economic basics.

As a rough planning guide, English and quantitative ability together usually account for about half the paper, with the remaining marks spread across reasoning, knowledge and banking awareness. The exact counts vary by batch and testing arrangement, which is why balanced preparation beats pattern-guessing. Candidates who pass the written stage are typically called for group discussion and/or interview, where communication and confidence are assessed.

Section-Wise Syllabus

English

Reading comprehension passages, vocabulary (synonyms, antonyms, idioms in context), sentence correction, and grammar — tenses, prepositions, articles, subject–verb agreement, active/passive voice and narration. English is a career skill in a bank, not just a test section; prepare it properly with our English preparation guide.

Quantitative Ability

Percentages, ratio and proportion, averages, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, partnership, time and work, time and distance, number systems, basic algebra, and reading tables and graphs. Master the standard methods topic by topic using our math preparation guide, then practise against the clock.

IQ and Analytical Reasoning

Number and letter series, coding–decoding, analogies, blood relations, direction sense, seating arrangements and statement-based logic. These questions look unfamiliar the first time and routine the fiftieth time — volume of practice is the entire secret. Start with the worked examples in our IQ and reasoning preparation guide.

General Knowledge, Current Affairs and Banking Awareness

Pakistan's history, geography and constitutional basics; major national and international current affairs; sports and honours; and banking awareness — functions of the State Bank of Pakistan, types of accounts, basic finance terms and Pakistan economy indicators at a general level. Build the habit with the current affairs preparation method and revise terms from the banking concepts explainer.

Negative Marking and Attempt Strategy

One detail decides how you should attempt the paper, and it is announced in the test instructions rather than in the advertisement: whether wrong answers are penalised. Policies differ between batches and testing arrangements, so never assume. If negative marking applies — commonly a fraction of a mark deducted per wrong answer in tests that use it — random guessing becomes expensive, and the correct strategy is to guess only when you can eliminate at least two options, which turns a blind one-in-four chance into a genuine one-in-two. If there is no penalty, leaving a question blank is simply a wasted opportunity, and you should attempt everything, saving the final minutes for your best guesses on whatever remains. Either way, work in passes: answer everything you know on the first pass, mark the questions that need thought, and return to them on the second pass with the clock you have left. Keep your attempt order deliberate — many candidates do English first while the mind is fresh, then quantitative, then reasoning, then the knowledge sections, which depend least on a tired brain. Practise this exact routine in your timed sessions, including reading the instructions page for a full minute before you start the clock, so that on the real day the strategy is a habit rather than a decision.

How to Prepare for NBP Tests

Take a timed diagnostic first — the topic quizzes on our NBP page will show exactly where you stand in each section. Then study in the same proportion as the paper: heavy blocks for English and quantitative work, daily short sets for IQ, and a fixed daily slot for current affairs and banking awareness. Keep a mistake notebook; NBP tests repeat question types relentlessly, so every error you analyse is a mark recovered. In the final ten days, switch to full-length timed tests — our full test reproduces the pressure of the real sitting — and revise your notebooks instead of learning anything new. Finally, read the test day tips and prepare your documents early. The MTO and OG-II seats go to candidates who are consistently solid across all five sections, and that consistency is built in weeks, not in the last night.
