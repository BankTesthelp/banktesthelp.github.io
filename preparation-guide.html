How to Prepare for Bank Tests in Pakistan

Bank recruitment tests in Pakistan — whether for NBP, SBP, or commercial banks like HBL, MCB and UBL — follow a predictable format: 80 or so multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes, spread across English, mathematics, reasoning, general knowledge, current affairs and banking. The good news is that this format rewards structured preparation far more than raw talent. With four to six weeks of focused study, most graduates can raise their score from failing to comfortably above the typical 60% passing mark. This guide lays out exactly how.

Weeks 1–2: Build the Foundations

The first two weeks are for learning, not speed. Start by diagnosing yourself: take one full-length test under real conditions and record your section-wise scores. Most candidates discover two weak sections and four acceptable ones — that diagnosis shapes everything that follows.

Then work through each section systematically. For English, revise core grammar (tenses, articles, prepositions, subject-verb agreement) and start a daily habit of ten new vocabulary words with synonyms and antonyms. For mathematics, master percentages, ratios, averages, profit and loss, and interest — these five areas produce the majority of bank-test math questions. In reasoning, learn the standard patterns for series, coding-decoding and blood relations. For general knowledge and banking, begin compiling short notes: Pakistan studies facts, SBP functions, banking terms and basic economics definitions.

Spend about two to three hours a day, rotating sections so nothing goes stale. End each study session with a short quiz on what you just covered — retrieval practice on this site is built for exactly this purpose.

Weeks 3–4: Per-Section Strategy

Now shift from learning to drilling. Each section needs its own approach. In English, practice error-spotting and sentence-correction sets; these reward grammar rules you can memorize. In mathematics, learn shortcut methods — fraction-to-percentage conversions, approximate calculations and ratio tricks — because the test gives you roughly one minute per question and long multiplication is a luxury you cannot afford.

Reasoning questions should be attacked by pattern type: when you see a series, your brain should cycle through difference, ratio, alternate and interlocking patterns automatically. That reflex only comes from volume — solve dozens of series until the patterns feel obvious. For current affairs, read national news and economic headlines daily, focusing on the last six to twelve months. For banking and finance, memorize the key terms cold: markup, collateral, LC, deposits, the policy rate and the functions of SBP and commercial banks. This section has the narrowest syllabus on the paper, so it is the cheapest source of marks.

By the end of week four, aim for at least 60% on every individual topic quiz.

Week 5: Full Tests and Time Management

Week five belongs to full-length tests — at least four of them. Sit for 80 questions in 90 minutes with no interruptions, no phone and no pausing the clock. Time management is a skill, not a personality trait, and it is built here.

Develop a question strategy before you open the paper. Attempt your strongest section first to build confidence, then rotate through the rest. Mark questions you are unsure about and move on — a stuck question costs two easy questions elsewhere. In the final ten minutes, review the marked ones. Most bank tests in Pakistan do not penalize wrong answers heavily, so leaving questions blank is rarely wise; when in doubt, make an educated guess after eliminating the obviously wrong options.

After each mock, spend as long analyzing as you did writing. Log every mistake in a notebook by section and by cause: concept gap, careless error or time pressure. Your revision plan writes itself from this log.

Week 6: Revision and Consolidation

The final week is not for new material. Revisit your mistake notebook daily. Re-drill your weakest section with topic quizzes. Take one last full test around day three or four, then taper — light revision only in the final two days. Cramming new topics at the end increases anxiety without adding marks.

Also use this week to read our test day tips: check your documents, plan your route to the test center and sort out sleep. Logistics failures cause more lost marks than hard questions do.

The Mindset That Wins

Bank tests are designed to be finished, not perfected. Candidates who clear them share three habits: they practice under time pressure, they review their mistakes instead of hiding from them, and they stay consistent for weeks instead of intense for days. You do not need to be a genius — you need to be prepared.

Start today with the topic quizzes, take your first full test this weekend, and work the plan. The passing mark is 60% — entirely within reach.
