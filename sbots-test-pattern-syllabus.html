SBOTS 29th Batch Test Pattern and Syllabus 2026

Before you open a single book, you should know exactly what the SBOTS written test usually looks like: which sections it contains, where the marks are concentrated, and which topics examiners return to batch after batch. This page lays out the typical SBOTS 29th batch test pattern and a subject-wise syllabus you can prepare against. One honest note before we begin: the State Bank of Pakistan announces the official scheme in its own advertisement, and details can shift slightly from batch to batch. Treat everything below as the established, typical pattern of recent SBOTS batches — not as the official paper — and always confirm the final details in the official advertisement.

The Typical Pattern at a Glance

Recent SBOTS written tests have generally been a single MCQ-based paper of about 100 questions, completed in roughly two hours, divided into three broad sections of comparable weight. As a working guide, candidates usually plan around this distribution:

  • English Composition — roughly one third of the paper. The largest single block in most batches, and the section where the merit list is often decided.
  • Quantitative and Analytical Ability — roughly one third. Arithmetic plus reasoning, where speed and accuracy matter equally.
  • General Knowledge, Current Affairs and Pakistan Economy — roughly one third. Pakistan studies, economics basics, banking awareness and recent events.

Because the three sections carry similar weight, the pattern punishes one-sided preparation. A candidate who is brilliant at mathematics but weak in English starts the paper with a third of the marks already at risk. Our complete SBOTS preparation guide turns this pattern into a six-week study plan; this page gives you the syllabus to feed into that plan.

Section 1: English Composition — Syllabus

English in SBOTS is a test of precision, not literature. The recurring topic areas are:

  • Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms and word meanings in context. Build a daily word list and revise it cumulatively.
  • Grammar and sentence correction: subject–verb agreement, tenses, articles, prepositions, pronouns, active and passive voice, direct and indirect speech, and spotting the grammatically wrong part of a sentence.
  • Sentence completion: choosing the word or phrase that completes a sentence correctly and logically.
  • Reading comprehension: a passage followed by questions on its main idea, details, inference and vocabulary-in-context.

Prepare with rules plus volume: learn a grammar rule, then immediately solve questions on it. Our English preparation guide organises this process, and the topic quizzes on the SBP page let you drill each sub-topic separately.

Section 2: Quantitative and Analytical Ability — Syllabus

The quantitative half covers school-level arithmetic applied at speed: percentages, ratios and proportions, averages, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, partnership, time and work, time and distance, basic algebra, and simple data interpretation from tables and charts. Nothing here requires advanced mathematics — it requires fast, error-free method. Learn the shortcut for each topic, but understand the full method first, because examiners vary the numbers to defeat memorised tricks.

The analytical half tests structured thinking: number and letter series, coding and decoding, seating and ordering arrangements, statement-and-conclusion and statement-and-assumption questions, and small logic puzzles. These questions reward practice volume more than any other area; patterns become visible only after you have solved many of them. Work through the quantitative topics in our math preparation guide and then mix arithmetic and reasoning in timed sets.

Section 3: General Knowledge, Current Affairs and Economy — Syllabus

This section connects the test to the institution you are joining. The recurring areas are Pakistan studies (history, geography, constitutional basics), the functions and structure of the State Bank of Pakistan, Pakistan economy fundamentals (budget, trade, inflation, remittances, agriculture and industry), banking and finance terminology, and current affairs from the months immediately before the test — national events, major international developments, sports and honours. Because the employer is the central bank, economic and banking awareness carries more weight here than in most commercial-bank tests, so do not treat this section as a light add-on. Our current affairs preparation method shows how to cover it in twenty minutes a day.

How to Prepare Against This Pattern

Start with a diagnosis: attempt the dedicated 60-question SBOTS practice set in one timed sitting and record your score in each section separately. Then divide your study time in the same proportion as the paper — roughly a third each — with extra time on whichever section your diagnostic exposed as weakest. Drill topic by topic using the topic quizzes, keep a written notebook of every mistake and its cause, and finish your preparation with full-length tests under exam conditions. The free SBOTS Batch 29 preparation book (PDF) gives you 400 original solved MCQs organised by section, which matches this pattern-based approach exactly.

The SBOTS pattern is demanding but predictable. Candidates who prepare section by section, in proportion to the marks, consistently outperform candidates who simply study harder in one favourite subject. Know the pattern, cover the syllabus, and practise under time — that is the whole formula.
