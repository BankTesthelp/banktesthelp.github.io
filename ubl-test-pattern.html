UBL OG-IV / BSO / RE Test Pattern and Preparation

United Bank Limited hires fresh and early-career talent into several entry-level roles, and three titles appear again and again in its advertisements: Officer Grade-IV (OG-IV), Branch Services Officer (BSO) and Retail Executive (RE). Each role has its own flavour — OG-IV is a general officer track, the BSO focuses on branch operations and services, and the RE is a sales-and-relationship role for retail banking products — but their written screening tests are built from the same familiar bank-test ingredients. This page explains the typical test pattern for these UBL roles, the syllabus under each section, and a practical way to prepare. The bank's official advertisement for any specific hiring round remains the final word; the pattern below is the typical structure these tests follow.

The Typical Test Pattern

UBL entry-level screening tests are generally MCQ-based and timed, drawn from five areas:

  • English Language — comprehension, grammar, vocabulary.
  • Quantitative Ability — arithmetic and basic data handling.
  • IQ and Analytical Reasoning — series, analogies, logic.
  • General Knowledge and Current Affairs — Pakistan and the wider world.
  • Banking and Business Awareness — basic banking terms, products and the role applied for.

Tests for these roles are usually speed-oriented: a moderate number of questions against a tight clock, so the deciding skill is answering straightforward questions quickly and correctly rather than solving a few very hard ones. Depending on the role and round, short-listed candidates may face further assessment and interviews, where communication and customer orientation matter — especially for BSO and RE positions.

Section-Wise Syllabus

English

Reading comprehension, synonyms and antonyms, sentence correction, common grammar (tenses, prepositions, subject–verb agreement) and sentence completion. For customer-facing roles, clear everyday English is the point. Follow the routine in our English preparation guide and drill from the UBL page.

Quantitative Ability

Percentages, ratios and proportions, averages, profit and loss, simple interest, basic algebra, and interpretation of small tables and charts. Branch work involves figures all day — charges, targets, balances — and the test reflects that. Our math preparation guide covers each topic with worked examples.

IQ and Analytical Reasoning

Number and letter series, coding–decoding, analogies, odd one out, blood relations and short logic sets. See the worked examples in our IQ and reasoning preparation guide, then practise in timed sets.

General Knowledge, Current Affairs and Banking Awareness

Pakistan studies basics, major current events, sports, and everyday banking: account types, cards, digital banking channels, and what branch officers actually do. Keep a light daily current-affairs habit using the current affairs method, and revise core terms from our banking concepts explainer.

Role-Specific Preparation Notes

OG-IV applicants should prepare the full pattern evenly, since the officer track draws on every section. BSO applicants benefit from extra attention to accuracy-based work — arithmetic care, detail-spotting and banking operations basics — because the role is operations-heavy. RE applicants should expect the same written pattern but should also prepare for interview questions about sales attitude, product knowledge and dealing with customers, since the role is target-driven. In all three cases the written test is the first filter: clear it comfortably and the rest of the process opens up.

What Test Day Usually Looks Like

Expect a formal, tightly run process. You will typically report well before the start time with your roll number slip or admit card and your original CNIC; latecomers are usually not accommodated, so plan your route with a generous margin. Seating is assigned, mobile phones are not permitted in the hall, and the invigilators explain the marking rules before the paper begins — listen carefully, because whether wrong answers carry a penalty decides how aggressively you should guess. Some hiring rounds are conducted on computer at designated centres and others on paper with an answer sheet; the preparation is identical either way, but if your test is computer-based, spend the first minute getting comfortable with the on-screen timer and the review or flag option, which lets you return to skipped questions at the end. Budget your time by section rather than by the whole paper: decide in advance, for example, that English gets a fixed share of the clock, and move on when that share is spent even if two questions remain. Candidates rarely fail these tests on knowledge alone — they fail by spending twelve minutes rescuing three hard questions while twenty easy ones sat unanswered behind them.

A Simple Preparation Routine

Start by attempting the recent-pattern UBL practice set in one timed sitting and note your section scores honestly. Over the following weeks, alternate English and quantitative study days, solve a short IQ set daily, and keep current affairs to a fixed twenty-minute slot. Practise topic by topic through the topic quizzes, write down every mistake with its reason, and move to full-length timed tests in the final week using our full test. Before the day, read the test day tips so logistics never cost you marks. UBL's tests reward candidates who are quick, accurate and prepared across the board — exactly what a few disciplined weeks of pattern-based practice will make you.
